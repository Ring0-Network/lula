## Proof and security scope

- **PROVED by manual relational argument, scoped:** the chain/tree reconstruction statement at lines 286–290 follows from the source's addressed chain completions, endpoint compression and matching authentication paths. The claim quantifies over successfully generated keys, honest cache contents and terminating searches. It does not assert that every key-generation or signing request returns.
- **PROVED by manual source comparison, scoped:** transparency at lines 309–319 compares the complete honest top cache or its absent representation against recomputation from counter zero. Source key generation stores the smallest accepted counter for the seed-derived child value (lines 535–541); signing reads cache material but does not update it (lines 601–667). The child root is independent of which message routes through its address. Removing a whole configured cache layer leaves recomputation of the same node and canonical counter. The generalization at line 316 explicitly requires a cached top segment and honest remaining entries.
- **PROVED by manual coupling, conditional:** lines 323–335 preserve the completed-call transcript, permanent queried-message set, public key, verifier and common hash interface. Opaque sequential snapshot handles require bookkeeping only. The simulation preserves signing-query count and adds no adversarial hash queries; it still charges simulator time, separately accounts honest signing work and assumes an applicable bound for the exact reference signer. No claim equates the much larger cache-miss work with hot-cache work.
- **Correctly delimited partiality:** lines 126, 293 and 323 explicitly exclude mid-call restoration, timing, crashes, partial outputs and explicit failure replies. Both source counter loops use a `u32` counter with no accepted-counter existence guarantee. In the release profile, a failed full scan can cycle after wrapping; debug overflow can panic. Neither constitutes a successful signature return, and neither is promoted by the paper to total correctness or a finite worst-case runtime.
- **Correctly delimited corruption:** lines 282 and 306 say the fallback accepts an alternative valid counter without checking minimality. A changed valid same-message signature is a transparency counterexample, not an EUF-CMA win. The honest-restoration theorem makes no arbitrary-cache-integrity claim. The accepted-counter antichain statement at line 301 is correct; lines 301–303 explicitly leave inversions, collisions, multi-target behavior and composition outside that elementary fact.
- **Correctly conditional objective:** lines 55 and 115–121 and `operating-model.tex:2,21` distinguish the intended `2^40` total signing-query objective from a concrete security level or certified capacity. The 132.6 figure is a classical per-raw-candidate FORS component exponent under independent uniform selections. It is not a full 128-bit statement, a quantum bound or a NIST category claim.
- **Correctly limited novelty and comparisons:** lines 59, 81–91 acknowledge established stateless architecture, caching, compression, varying layers and canonical-counter prior code. The claimed contribution is the specified integration and conditional rollback transfer. No generic novelty theorem is asserted. `comparison-results.tex:17` explicitly says no real SHRINCS forgery was run. The frozen-state example at lines 344–365 is a generic construction separating two implications, not an attack on LULA I or SHRINCS.

The new private research bounds are not needed to validate this paper's deliberately conditional mechanism claims. No private theorem, scalar, game, or security-category assertion was imported into the manuscript by this review.

## Algorithm and byte-interface audit

Sign transmits the randomizer, FORS counter, 17 real FORS proofs, five WOTS counters, chain values and paths. CacheRestore's accepted-cache path and rejected-counter fallback match source behavior within its honest-cache input contract.

The 22-byte addressed framing, first-16-byte truncation, 64-byte prepared SHA prefix, raw-message byte strings, MSB-first bit extraction and big-endian counters match source.
